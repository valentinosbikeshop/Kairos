# Evidence-Based Maximal Strength & Powerlifting

This skill dictates programming rules, neuromuscular recovery timelines, and movement execution heuristics for maximizing 1RM (one-repetition maximum) strength and power development.

---

## 1. Neuromuscular Physiology vs. Hypertrophy

While hypertrophy increases muscle cross-sectional area (PCSA), maximal strength ($1\text{RM}$) is governed by **neural drive**:
1. **High-Threshold Motor Unit Recruitment**: Henneman's size principle dictates that lifting loads $\ge 82\text{--}85\% \text{ 1RM}$ immediately recruits all Type IIa and IIx fast-twitch fibers.
2. **Rate Coding**: The firing frequency of action potentials dispatched from spinal motor neurons.
3. **Inter-muscular & Intra-muscular Coordination**: Agonist-antagonist co-activation optimization and synergistic stabilizer stiffening.

---

## 2. Phosphocreatine (PCr) Recovery Kinetics

In maximal strength sets ($1\text{--}5 \text{ reps}$ at $\ge 85\% \text{ 1RM}$), the anaerobic alactic system (ATP-PCr) is the exclusive energy source.
- **Grgic et al. (2022)**:
  - At **60 seconds**: Only $\sim 55\text{--}65\%$ of phosphocreatine is replenished.
  - At **120 seconds**: Approximately $75\text{--}80\%$ is restored.
  - At **180 to 300 seconds (3 to 5 minutes)**: $>95\%$ of ATP and PCr stores are fully resynthesized.
- **Rule**: Strength blocks require **3 to 5 minutes of rest** between heavy sets. Entering a heavy set prior to 3 minutes causes failure due to chemical substrate depletion rather than neuromuscular limit.

## 3. Velocity-Loss Thresholds & Proximity to Failure

### 3.1 Velocity-Based Training (VBT)
- **Pareja-Blanco et al. (2017 & 2020)**: Compared groups training with a **20% velocity loss** versus a **40% velocity loss** (grinding to near-failure):
  - The **20% velocity loss group** gained **significantly more maximal strength and explosive power (jump height)** with half the total repetitions.
  - The **40% velocity loss group** experienced excessive mechanical and hormonal fatigue, slower muscle recovery (up to 72h), and conversion of fast Type IIx fibers to slower Type IIa fibers.
- **Rule**: In strength training, stop sets when movement speed visibly decelerates by $\sim 20\%$. This correlates to an **RPE of 7.5 to 8.5 (RIR 2 to 3)**.

### 3.2 Avoid Grinding to Failure in Multi-Joint Barbell Lifts
- Testing strength is not the same as building strength. Grinding 1RMs or failing squats/deadlifts induces severe central nervous system (CNS) depression without stimulating additional motor unit adaptation.

## 4. Periodization: DUP & Block Periodization

### 4.1 Daily Undulating Periodization (DUP)
Altering rep ranges and intensities on different days within the same week out-performs linear periodization for intermediate and advanced lifters:
- **Day A (Hypertrophy / Work Capacity)**: 3–4 sets of 8 reps @ 70% 1RM (RPE 7.5).
- **Day B (Power / Speed)**: 5–6 sets of 3 reps @ 75% 1RM (maximal concentric acceleration).
- **Day C (Maximal Strength)**: 3–5 sets of 3–5 reps @ 85% 1RM (RPE 8–8.5).

### 4.2 Deload Protocols
Every 4th or 5th week of intense strength training:
- Reduce total volume (sets) by **40–50%**.
- Maintain load intensity at **75–80%** to preserve neural skill.

## 5. Spinal Mechanics & Biomechanical Bracing (Dr. Stuart McGill)

When moving maximal axial loads (Squat, Deadlift, Overhead Press):
1. **Intra-Abdominal Pressure (IAP)**: Execute the **Valsalva Maneuver** against a 360-degree cylindrical brace (engaging transverse abdominis, internal/external obliques, quadratus lumborum, and pelvic floor).
2. **Neutral Spine Principle**: Avoid lumbar flexion or hyper-extension under load. Lumbar flexion during deadlift dramatically increases disc shear strain and herniation risk.
3. **Lat Engagement**: "Bend the bar" or squeeze armpits tight to engage the latissimus dorsi, pulling the humerus into external rotation and bracing the thoracolumbar fascia.
