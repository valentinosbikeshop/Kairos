# Recovery Physiology, Sleep Architecture & HRV Autoregulation

This skill governs post-exercise recovery monitoring, autonomic nervous system telemetry, sleep architecture optimization, and nutritional timing for sustained high performance.

---

## 1. Sleep Architecture & Neuro-Endocrine Adaptation

Based on neurophysiological findings synthesized by **Dr. Matthew Walker (UC Berkeley)** and **Dr. Andy Galpin**:

### 1.1 Slow-Wave Sleep (NREM Stage 3/4 - Deep Sleep)
- **Physiology**: Synchronized delta brainwaves ($0.5\text{--}4 \text{ Hz}$), muscle atonia, reduced core body temperature, and parasympathetic dominance.
- **Anabolic Function**: Over **95% of daily systemic Growth Hormone (GH)** pulses occur during slow-wave sleep. GH stimulates insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), driving connective tissue collagen synthesis and skeletal muscle repair.
- **Deprivation Cost**: Sleeping $<6$ hours for 2 consecutive nights reduces circulating testosterone by up to $15\%$ and increases circulating cortisol, promoting catabolism.

### 1.2 REM Sleep (Rapid Eye Movement)
- **Function**: Motor skill consolidation, synaptic pruning, reaction time optimization, and central motor drive restoration.
- **Athletic Relevance**: Learning complex technical movements (Olympic lifts, gymnastics levers, double unders) relies heavily on REM sleep to consolidate cerebellum motor pathways.

## 2. Heart Rate Variability (HRV) & Autonomic Telemetry

Based on computational sports physiology by **Marco Altini PhD**:

### 2.1 Metric of Choice: RMSSD
The gold standard biomarker for cardiac autonomic regulation is **RMSSD** (Root Mean Square of Successive Differences between adjacent RR intervals):

$$\text{RMSSD} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N - 1} \sum_{i=1}^{N - 1} (RR_{i+1} - RR_{i})^2}$$

- **Vagal Proxy**: RMSSD directly reflects parasympathetic (vagus nerve) activity modulating the sinoatrial node. Higher values indicate an adaptable, recovered nervous system.

### 2.2 Rolling 7-Day Baseline & Dynamic Autoregulation
- Daily single-value HRV is noisy and meaningless without context.
- Maintain a **rolling 7-day baseline** and establish the athlete’s **Normal Band** (Baseline Mean $\pm 0.75 \times \text{Standard Deviation}$):
  - **Within Normal Band**: Autonomic equilibrium. Execute scheduled hard training (RPE 8–9, Zone 4/5).
  - **Below Normal Band (Depressed HRV + High Resting HR)**: Sympathetic overreach or systemic stress (infection, alcohol, poor sleep). Auto-regulate: reduce volume by 25–40% or switch to Zone 1/2 recovery.
  - **Abnormally Elevated HRV + Low Resting HR**: Parasympathetic saturation / hyper-recovery (frequently observed prior to functional overreaching exhaustion). Monitor closely.

## 3. Hydration & Protein Timing Science

### 3.1 Protein Boluses & Leucine Trigger
- **Daily Target**: **$1.6\text{ to }2.2\text{ g/kg of bodyweight}$** (Morton et al., 2018 meta-analysis).
- **Distribution**: Distribute into **3 to 4 feeding windows of $0.40\text{ to }0.55\text{ g/kg}$**, spaced 3 to 5 hours apart.
- **The Leucine Threshold**: Each meal should supply $\ge 2.5\text{--}3.0\text{g}$ of the essential amino acid leucine to trigger intracellular **Sestrin2-mTORC1** activation.

### 3.2 Electrolyte Osmolarity & Plasma Volume Preservation
- Sweating causes loss of water and sodium ($500\text{ to }1500\text{ mg Na}^+ / \text{liter}$).
- Drinking pure demineralized water during intense training dilutes plasma sodium, leading to hyponatremia and accelerated fatigue.
- Consume **$500\text{ to }1000\text{ mg}$ of elemental sodium** with water during sessions exceeding 60 minutes or in high ambient temperatures to sustain cardiac output and prevent blood volume reduction.
